How to Connect a Custom Domain to a Cloudflare Website

Understand DNS, nameservers, conflicting records and HTTPS before connecting a domain to your static site.

By Gagana Chathuranga · Updated September 22, 2026

After publishing a website, you may want visitors to use your own domain instead of a hosting provider's default address. This guide explains the main concepts and the safe order of operations for a website hosted on Cloudflare.

What is the difference between a domain and hosting?

A domain is a human-readable address such as example.com. Hosting serves the actual HTML, images and other files. DNS records connect the domain name to the service that should answer requests. Buying a domain alone does not automatically publish your website.

1. Make sure the website already works

Publish the site and open its temporary hosting address first. Check the homepage, images and navigation. This separates website-file problems from domain-configuration problems: if the temporary address is broken, fix the deployment before editing DNS.

2. Check where your DNS is managed

Your registrar is the company where you registered the domain. Your authoritative DNS provider may be the registrar or another service such as Cloudflare. The domain's nameservers determine where its active DNS records are managed.

If you have moved DNS management to Cloudflare, use the exact nameservers assigned to your domain in your Cloudflare dashboard. Do not copy someone else's nameservers from a tutorial. Nameserver changes may take time to be recognized by resolvers.

3. Attach the domain through the hosting project

Open the project that serves your website and find its domain or routing settings. Add the hostname you want visitors to use. For this site, the main hostname is gaganachathuranga.com.lk. Cloudflare's Worker Custom Domain setup can associate that hostname with the Worker and manage the required routing.

Read any warning before confirming. The exact options depend on whether your site is deployed as a Worker with static assets or through another Cloudflare hosting workflow.

4. Resolve conflicting DNS records carefully

A hostname cannot always keep an old record pointing at another service while also being assigned to your new website. If Cloudflare reports a conflict, identify the record for the same hostname and check what service it currently supports.

Safety tip: Do not delete MX, TXT or unrelated subdomain records just to fix a website conflict. Those records may support email or domain verification. If you are unsure, save a screenshot or export your DNS records before making changes.

Once the conflicting website record has been resolved, retry the custom-domain setup and allow Cloudflare to create or manage the necessary record according to its prompts.

5. Verify HTTPS and both address forms

Open the site with https:// and confirm the browser shows your actual website. A certificate may need time to become active after setup. If you also want www to work, configure that hostname separately and choose whether it should serve the same content or redirect to your main domain.

Use one consistent primary address in your navigation links, canonical tags, sitemap and social profiles. This helps visitors and search engines avoid unnecessary duplicate addresses.

6. Test after every change

Check the homepage, one article, an image and a downloadable file. If the old placeholder page still appears, compare the active DNS records with your hosting project's domain configuration. If the temporary hosting address works but the custom domain does not, the issue is likely in the domain or routing setup rather than your HTML.

Common questions

Should I buy hosting from the domain registrar too?

Not necessarily. You can buy the domain from one provider and host the website with another.

Will changing DNS delete my website files?

No. DNS controls how visitors reach a service; it does not itself delete the files stored by your hosting provider. However, an incorrect DNS change can make the site temporarily unreachable.

Do I need to change nameservers for every site update?

No. Once the domain and hosting are connected, ordinary article or design updates usually require only a new deployment of website files.
